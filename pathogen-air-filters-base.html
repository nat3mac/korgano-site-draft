The Challenge

Why we are developing Pathogen Air Filters

Environmental shifts and the limitations of conventional filtration create an urgent need for next-generation indoor air safety.

Rising temperatures are expanding the geographic zones where viruses and bacteria thrive, increasing the frequency and severity of airborne disease outbreaks—particularly in enclosed environments.

Published Peer-Reviewed Literature

A Chilling Truth

“Published research shows that pathogens can remain virulent on traditional air filters for extended periods, facilitating their spread”

In Survival of Microorganisms on HEPA Filters (Mittal et al., Health Protection Agency, UK), researchers demonstrated that captured microorganisms—including spore formers, moulds, and bacteriophages—survived on HEPA filter material under normal operating airflow conditions with no significant loss of viability, creating potential for exposure or re-aerosolization.

Mittal et al., Survival of Microorganisms on HEPA Filters (Health Protection Agency, UK)

Core Science

Nanotechnology at the Forefront

KorganoTech is a nanotechnology-based air filter that uses known antimicrobial bioactive metal particles (BMPs), such as silver, copper, and metal oxides, embedded in an ultra-high surface area, porous nanowire web to capture pathogens of all sizes. When BMPs interact with the captured pathogens to form metal ions (created via dissolution of BMPs) that damage the pathogens' proteins and DNA, preventing them from growing and reproducing.

Ultra-High Surface Area Architecture: Multi-layer Pathogen Filter + Pre-Filter (50–150 nm porous nanowire web, ~1/1000 of a human hair)
Bioactive Metal Particles (BMPs): Metal ions (Ag⁺, Cu²⁺, Zn²⁺, Fe³⁺, Mg²⁺) induce membrane, protein, and DNA damage
Mission & Origin

Our motivation to pursue pathogen air filters

At the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, many of us relied on traditional air filters to keep us safe by providing clean air. However, published research shows that these filters are largely ineffective against airborne pathogens such as viruses, bacteria, and other microorganisms. While they may capture particles, traditional filters do not have the ability to deactivate or destroy pathogens—unless paired with additional technologies like UV purification or heat sterilization.

Over time, trapped pathogens can accumulate and potentially be re-released into the air, contaminating homes, buildings, and vehicles. This critical gap in air safety inspired us to develop KorganoTech Pathogen Air Filters, with the mission to protect lives, reduce costs, and improve public health.

Today, clean air matters more than ever because airborne diseases and viral outbreaks have had devastating effects on both public health and the global economy. We believe that investing in advanced air purification is no longer a luxury—it’s a necessity.
